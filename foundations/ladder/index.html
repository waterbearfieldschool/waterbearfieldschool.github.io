From Kit to Capacity

Most people who have thought about resilience at all have assembled a kit — a radio, a few weeks of water and food, warm clothes for a cold house, lights and batteries. That kit is right, and it is worth having. This page is about what comes after it.

The kit buys two to four weeks. The capacity takes about ten years. Which is not an argument against the kit. The kit is what buys the time.

A stocked shelf is consumed and then it is gone, and resupply comes from the same system whose interruption created the problem. Nothing has been built. What we are actually after is different in kind: productive capacity that survives being drawn upon — a garden rather than a pantry, a skill rather than a spare, a woodlot rather than a woodpile.

That takes time, which is the whole reason to start now, while nothing has happened and the kit is still in the closet. You cannot begin the decade during the two weeks.

The four rungs

Borrowed from expedition practice, where a base camp supports progressive camps above it, each supplied by the one below.

Rung Where you are The question it answers
Camp 1 Everything carried in. Nothing produced. How many days of supply?
Camp 2 Still carrying, but thinking about sources rather than stores. How long between resupplies?
Camp 3 Infrastructure exists. The site produces something measurable. What does it produce?
Camp 4 Output exceeds input. The system regenerates. What keeps going with nothing brought in?

The move from camp 3 to camp 4 is the one people underestimate. Homesteads under two years old typically produce a quarter or less of their household's food; those a decade in report around seventy per cent. A decade, not a season.

One question sorts almost everything

Can this be consumed without being destroyed? Stored calories fail. A productive system passes.

Two follow-ups we use throughout:

  • The truck test. What still arrives by truck? A garden run on bought fertiliser, bought seed and bought starts looks like production and behaves like consumption.
  • The seed-capital rule. What must never be eaten, written down before the hungry season. Jamestown survived because a rule forbade slaughtering the breeding stock — seed capital always looks like food when you are hungry.

Camp 1, made concrete

Camp 1 isn't the pantry alone. It is a kit spanning every domain at once, and here is roughly how long each part of it lasts.

Domain The camp 1 item Runs out in
Water Stored jugs; a filter cartridge Days stored; the cartridge is thousands of gallons, then gone
Food The pantry Two weeks to three months
Heating Propane; a cord of wood Weeks — a New England season is three to five cords
Energy Headlamps, cells, a power bank Days to weeks. A panel extends it; the battery still ages out
Communication A radio on batteries Days without charging — and repeaters may be down anyway
Air Boxed filters Months
Clothing Emergency warm layers Years — the happy outlier
Shelter The house as it stands Until something breaks that you cannot fix

The ladder, domain by domain

Each of our foundations has its own version of the climb — and its own honest ceiling. We think naming the ceilings is the most useful thing on this page.

Water camp 4 reachable

Stored jugs, to a filter plus knowing which roof and stream are viable, to catchment with a measured yield and filtration you can rebuild from sand, gravel and char — and the ability to test whether the water is safe. Camp 4 is the closed loop, including the char you burned yourself.

Still arriving by truck: cartridges, chlorine, fittings.  •  Don't consume: the test kit and the source survey — without them the rest is guesswork.

Food camp 4, a decade out

The pantry, to a garden run on bought inputs, to beds with measured output plus season extension and real preservation capacity, to saved seed and fertility from compost and nitrogen fixation. This is the domain the ten-year figure comes from.

Still arriving by truck: fertiliser, seed, feed, potting mix.  •  Don't consume: the seed, and the breeding stock.

Shelter camp 4, a decade out

The house you have, to fixing what breaks, to building — framing, sheds, cold frames — with a salvage stream running. Camp 4 is local material, maintenance that outlives the builder, and the real threshold: you can teach someone else to build. A well-built structure is still producing shelter in eighty years.

Still arriving by truck: fasteners, sheet goods, roofing.  •  Don't consume: the tools, and the structural lumber.

Heating & Cooling camp 4 reachable

Thermostat and propane, to insulation and a woodstove, to a local firewood supply, thermal mass and a root cellar doing the cooling. Camp 4 is coppice — a woodlot cut on rotation, which is the cleanest example on this whole page. A woodpile is consumed and gone; a rotation regenerates indefinitely.

Still arriving by truck: propane, chainsaw fuel, foam board.  •  Don't consume: don't cut below rotation.

Energy hard ceiling

Grid and generator, to panels you can wire and maintain, to diagnosis, repair and re-purposing — charge controllers, battery management, salvaged cells, matching load to source.

Camp 4 is not reachable, and we would rather say so. There is no near-term local path to semiconductors or photovoltaic cells. The honest top of this ladder is reduced demand, plus mechanical, thermal and gravitational energy that needs no chip at all. Any programme promising energy independence is selling something.

Still arriving by truck: effectively everything.  •  Don't consume: don't cannibalise the working system for parts.

Communication hard ceiling, with a floor

Phone and internet, to radios you bought and nodes you flashed, to actually running a network — mesh, repeaters, propagation, routing around failure.

Same semiconductor wall as energy, with one real exception: the pre-transistor tier genuinely reaches camp 4. Crystal sets, antennas built from wire, and the knowledge to use them. Which is why we teach crystal radio — not as nostalgia, but because it is the rung that survives the constraint.

Transport camp 4 reachable

Car and fuel, to a bike you can patch, to building and repairing trailers, wheels and drivetrains from scavenged stock. Camp 4 is already here: human power regenerates and the fuel is food. A bicycle is repairable indefinitely with hand tools, and its wear items are a short, substitutable list.

Still arriving by truck: bearings, chain, tyres.  •  Don't consume: don't strip the working bike for the project bike.

Fabrication camp 3 is the honest top

Buy it, to repair it, to make it from bought stock — welding, casting, metalwork. Camp 4 is tools that make tools, fed by salvage; casting from scrap aluminium is the archetype.

Its own ceiling is moderate, but fabrication raises the ceiling of every other domain on this page, which is worth more than its own score.

Craft & Textiles camp 3 on volume

Buy clothes, to mending, to making from bought material, to fibre-to-cloth. The honest note: clothing a whole household from home production was recognised as nearly impossible in 1768 — by the people attempting it.

But this domain carries something the others don't. The homespun campaign worked to the degree it did because spinning bees made local production social, public and identity-bearing — towns competed against neighbouring towns. In every historical case, what held was the part that was social rather than policed. That is not a small thing to be in charge of.

Ecology camp 4 reachable

Nature as scenery, to identification, to reading the system — soil, succession, water movement, indicator species — to management: the land produces more because of your presence, not less. That is the regeneration threshold with nothing else attached to it.

Don't consume: don't forage a population below replacement.

Air camp 4 reachable

Bought purifier and filters, to a box filter you built and sized yourself, to measuring — carbon dioxide and particulates, diagnosing a space, specifying the fix. Camp 4 is ventilation and siting that need no filter at all.

Which is worth stating directly: meeting outdoors is camp 4 in this domain. It is the one place on this page where the top of the ladder is also the cheapest option, and we lead with it for that reason rather than in spite of it.

Hospitality no ceiling

Buying the gathering, to cooking for a group, to feeding and sheltering people in the field. Camp 4 is relationship as infrastructure, and it costs nothing but time.

Plymouth's bridge across the gap was not stored provisions — it was a local relationship and the instruction that came with it. Hospitality is not an amenity bolted onto a resilience programme. It is what carries the whole thing through the first years, when nothing produces yet.

Libraries & Learning camp 4 reachable

Buy the curriculum, to adapting bought materials, to authoring your own — writing and printing the booklet. Camp 4 is a school that produces rather than consumes, and that reproduces teachers, not only students.

The substitution that matters is authorship, not medium: paper instead of screens is nostalgia unless somebody locally can write what goes on it. And on the historical record this isn't one domain among many — it is the engine. Every serious attempt at localisation built a school, and built it early, because without a way to rebuild removed skills there is no camp 3 in anything.

Where the ladder stops

Ceiling Domains
Camp 4 genuinely reachable Water · Heating · Ecology · Transport · Air · Hospitality · Libraries
Camp 4, a decade out Food · Shelter
Camp 3 is the honest top Fabrication · Craft
Hard-capped below camp 4 Energy · Communication — the semiconductor wall

A programme claiming even progress across all of these would not be credible. We would rather say that water and firewood can go all the way, that chips cannot, and why — and be believed about the rest.

Two we are adding

Working the kit across every domain made two absences obvious, and both matter in a long outage:

  • Sanitation and waste — no flush, and fairly soon no sewer. Historically this is what actually harms people. The ladder is clean and teachable, from bucket-and-cover through composting systems and greywater, and it pairs naturally with water and ecology.
  • Health and medicine — first aid at camp 1, and the harder cliff, since prescriptions run out on a thirty-day cycle for a great many households. Camp 4 is out of reach here, the same as energy. Camps 2 and 3 are not.

Where to start

Not with energy. It is the most technical, the most legible, the most photogenic — and it has the hardest ceiling and the deepest import dependency of anything on this page.

Start where camp 4 is actually reachable: water and heating. Then find out what still arrives by truck, and write down what you have agreed not to consume.

Every workshop we run sits somewhere on this ladder. Most of them teach the step from camp 1 to camp 2, or camp 2 to camp 3 — the step from 3 to 4 needs land and years, which is what the longer projects are for. If you want to know which rung something is on, ask us. We will tell you, including when the answer is "lower than it looks."
